# Failed one-year target explanations: stock-by-stock audit

Reviewed September 12, 2026. Scope: all 154 failed Buy episodes (96 tickers) in the deployed SFA backtest and local `web/data/backtest-sfa.json`. Deployed JSON was retrieved directly and its failure count and key examples were checked against the local file. This is a common-sense and implementation audit, not newly completed historical causal research for 96 companies. No production code or published data was changed.

## Findings

- **The section overstates research coverage.** It calls every row a researched reason, but 151 of 154 episodes have no source links. Only LOW, KLAC, and CSCO have saved source-backed text; LOW and KLAC use evidence from the wrong decade.
- **Research is matched by ticker, not by episode.** LOW's November 30, 2006 recommendation is explained using fiscal-2024 sales. KLAC's November 28, 2011 recommendation cites a June 2025 report about export controls. Those explanations do not establish why the 2006–07 and 2011–12 targets failed. The hardcoded evidence text nevertheless says the episode window was reviewed. See `fairentry/backtest/evidence.py:612` and `config/target_failure_research.json`.
- **The comparison period is wrong.** `_target_failure_reasons` defaults to 1,095 days (`fairentry/backtest/evidence.py:609`), and its caller at line 957 never supplies 365. Three-year benchmark comparisons cannot be presented as explanations of a one-year miss. This affects every benchmark-based reason, including 84 underperformance labels and 22 sector labels; some episodes have both.
- **Price comparisons are presented as causes.** A stock trailing a benchmark says what happened, not why. A broad-market loss of at least 10% is converted into a claim about sector valuation multiples, although the evidence explicitly admits sector attribution needs review. There is no sector or valuation-multiple evidence in that rule. See `fairentry/backtest/evidence.py:643`.
- **Two different targets are mixed.** 'Target price was overly optimistic' is triggered by a practical target above 70%, even though this table measures a fixed 30% goal. CRUS (2013-09-03) uses 71.9%; LZB (2021-08-30) uses 85.6%. Seven episodes carry this label, two as the displayed primary reason. See `fairentry/backtest/evidence.py:651`.
- **An internal score is not causal proof.** PCLE (2003-07-01) and VSI (2014-03-03) get 'Valuation was previously too high' solely from a low internal valuation score. This may flag a hypothesis; it does not prove why the next year's price missed a threshold.
- **The table conceals evidence and secondary labels.** `web/backtest.html:168` renders only the first reason and source URLs, omitting the available evidence text, evidence status, and any secondary reason. Fourteen episodes have secondary reasons. The primary is determined by rule order, not a research-based ranking.
- **Duplicates are mostly repeated recommendations.** There are no duplicate ticker + start-date keys. TER and INTC each have five failed episodes; BBOX, TSCO, and LZB each have four. These represent different starts, often with overlapping holding windows; they should not be mistaken for independent companies or independent experiments.
- **A miss is not necessarily a loss.** ROST (2002-08-01) reached +29.71% during the first year and +30% after 379 days. It missed the chosen deadline by 14 days. That does not establish a company failure or justify inventing an adverse event.
- **Related evidence has a counting contradiction.** PCLE (2003-07-01) is correctly classified as a one-year failure in `fixed_30_evaluation`, but its `fixed_30_reason` says acquisition excluded it. The acquisition date is August 9, 2005, after its June 30, 2004 one-year deadline. This should be reconciled before showing the detailed explanation.
- **The queue repeats the matching flaw.** `fairentry/backtest/failure_research.py:53` considers any ticker in the registry covered, irrespective of the episode date. Saved findings are append-only by ticker, preventing a later episode-specific correction through the existing importer. HTTPS-only validation does not verify authority or that a source supports a claim.

## Plain language

| Current wording | Meaning / safer wording |
|---|---|
| Company-specific underperformance versus the benchmark | The stock did worse than the comparison index. This does not explain why. Show the actual index, returns, and matching one-year dates in a separate comparison column. |
| Cause not verified from available point-in-time evidence | We have not verified why it missed the target. |
| Sector-wide change reduced valuation multiples | Remove as a cause. Once recalculated for the right dates, the supported observation may be: The wider market fell during this year. |
| Target price was overly optimistic | Remove from this fixed-30% explanation. A different, higher target cannot explain this miss. |
| Customer inventory digestion and cautious IT spending... | Customers were still using equipment they had already bought and delayed new orders. Cisco's sales fell. Treat this as a reported business headwind, not proof of the sole cause. |

Suggested title: **Stocks that missed the +30% one-year target**. Separate **What the price did**, **What we know about the business**, and **Research status**. Use **Not yet verified** for absent causal research, instead of filling the business column with price-performance labels.
