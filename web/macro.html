How to read this dashboard

Growth and jobs describe the real economy. Inflation and rates affect purchasing power and borrowing costs. The yield curve reflects interest-rate expectations; financial conditions describe stress across funding markets. Valuation adds the price investors are paying for stocks.

These are descriptive observations, not a crash forecast, recession declaration or automatic trading signal. No combined risk score is assigned and stock scores receive zero macro weight. A rising reading is not always good or bad: read the explanation for each measure.

Sources and calculation methods

Economic series come from FRED, with originating-agency attribution in each source link. PCE inflation is calculated as (index / index in the same month last year − 1) × 100. GDP growth is already annualized by the source. “pp” means percentage points; 1 pp equals 100 basis points. A change from 4% to 5% is +1 pp, not +1%.

Historical ranges use all available source-frequency observations in the selected reference decade, including recessions and outliers. Mean is the arithmetic average; median is the middle observation; percentile cutoffs use linear interpolation. The pre-pandemic option is an alternative regime comparison, not a healthier or more correct standard. Claims are counts rather than a labor-force-adjusted rate. Each series uses its own scale.

Long-term direction compares the latest value with the nearest available observation on or before the date 1, 2, 5 or 10 years earlier. Missing comparable history is shown explicitly. Historical data may be revised; this is not a point-in-time backtest. Downloads are cached for one UTC day. Failed or stale data keeps its original date and loses current-condition coloring. Freshness limits: 7 days for daily series, 21 for weekly, 75 for unemployment, 100 for PCE and 180 from quarter-end for GDP. These are operational settings, not economic thresholds.
